

**LDR AUTOMATIC LIGHT CONTROL PROJECT**

**1\. Aim**

        To design and build an automatic light control system using an LDR (Light Dependent Resistor) that detects the surrounding light intensity and automatically switches an LED ON in darkness and OFF in daylight.

**2\. Problem Statement**

        When people are away from home during the evening or night, there may be no one available to switch the lights ON. Similarly, the light may not be required during the daytime.

Therefore, an automatic system is required to detect the surrounding light intensity and control the light without manual operation.

**3\. Proposed Solution**

        The proposed system uses an LDR sensor to detect the intensity of surrounding light.

During the daytime, sufficient light falls on the LDR, and the system keeps the LED OFF. When the surrounding light decreases during the evening or night, the LDR detects the darkness and the system automatically turns the LED ON.

This provides automatic lighting without the need for manual switching.

**4\. Components Required**

\- LDR Sensor  
\- LED  
\- Resistor  
\- Transistor  
\- Breadboard / Dot Board  
\- Power Supply / Battery  
\- Jumper Wires

**5\. Circuit Working**

    The LDR acts as a light-sensitive resistor whose resistance changes according to the surrounding light intensity.

When sufficient daylight falls on the LDR, its resistance changes and the circuit keeps the LED OFF.

When the surrounding environment becomes dark, the resistance of the LDR changes. This activates the switching circuit and turns the LED ON.

Therefore, the LED automatically responds to changes in the surrounding light level without requiring manual operation.

**6\. Procedure**

1\. Connect the components to the breadboard according to the circuit diagram.  
2\. Connect the LDR sensor to the sensing circuit.  
3\. Connect the LED with the correct polarity.  
4\. Connect the resistor to limit the LED current.  
5\. Connect the transistor as the switching element.  
6\. Connect the power supply to the circuit.  
7\. Check all connections carefully before switching ON the power supply.  
8\. Place the LDR in an area where it can sense the surrounding light.  
9\. Expose the LDR to light and observe that the LED remains OFF.  
10\. Cover the LDR or reduce the surrounding light and observe that the LED turns ON.

**7\. Applications**

💡 Automatic Home Lighting – Automatically controls lights based on daylight and darkness.

🌙 Night Lamps – Automatically turns ON the lamp when the surroundings become dark.

🚦 Street Lighting – Can be used for automatic switching of street lights.

🏠 Security Lighting – Provides automatic lighting around homes and buildings.

🔋 Energy Saving Systems – Helps avoid unnecessary lighting during daytime.

**8\. Advantages**

💡 Automatic ON/OFF operation.

⚡ Reduces unnecessary power consumption.

🔧 Simple and low-cost circuit.

🌙 Works automatically during night-time.

🏠 No need for manual switching.

🎓 Easy to build and understand.

**9\. Result**

The LDR-based automatic light control circuit was successfully built and tested.

The system successfully detected changes in the surrounding light intensity. The LED remained OFF during sufficient daylight and automatically turned ON when the surrounding environment became dark.

**10\. Conclusion**

This project demonstrates the practical use of an LDR as a light-sensing device for automatic lighting control. The system eliminates the need for manual switching and provides a simple, low-cost, and convenient solution for automatic lighting. It also provides a basic understanding of light sensing, resistance variation, and electronic switching.
